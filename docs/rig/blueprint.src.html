Max Day, 804 Hacks, Track 2

A county's water rule, running on a table

Two identical miniature cooling plants drink from one county supply on the same hot day. One runs the “water-efficient” hybrid rule. The other runs Max Day. A judge brings the heat wave. The hybrid's column rises through the red cap line. Max Day's stops on it.

Front view at the end of the demo. Water in the columns is the water each plant spent on the worst day. Scale: 1 mL in a column stands for 1,500 gallons. Two tubes feed each column.

Why this is the solution and not a prop

It moves water in time

Max Day doesn't use less water across the year. It spends the water in the hours where it saves the most power, so the worst day falls 42% at the same yearly water and the same peak power.

The law becomes a meter and a valve

The drafted condition caps daily water and requires reporting. On the rig, a meter measures every millilitre and the firmware shuts the valve at the cap, whatever the planner asks.

The judge controls the weather

The heat sensor reads the room. A hair dryer or a cup of hot coffee under it makes a heat wave, and the plan for the rest of the day changes live. Nothing is a recording.

The demo, one minute

The rig is beat three of the pitch. One simulated day runs in 60 seconds, 2.5 seconds per hour, on the measured weather of 24 June 2025 at Hanover County Airport.

  1. 0:00Press Run in the app“Two plants, one county supply, last June's hottest day.”
  2. 0:08Hand the judge a hot cup (or a hair dryer if one is around), held under the sensor“You're the heat wave.” The app shows the live temperature climb.
  3. 0:25Both valves open in the afternoonAmber LEDs light under each column while it spends water.
  4. 0:45The hybrid's column crosses the red line“That's the day the county can't spare.”
  5. 1:00Max Day's valve shuts on the line“Same day, same heat. It ran dry in the cheap hours and never raised the peak.”

How it works

  • The planner is the same tested engine the app uses: each simulated hour it re-plans the hours left, using the live temperature and the water already metered.
  • The interlock lives in the Arduino sketch: about 15 lines that close the Max Day valve once metered water reaches the cap. The hybrid side has none, which is the status quo.
  • The link is Chrome's Web Serial over the USB cable. The presenting laptop runs Chrome or Edge, since Firefox and Safari lack it.

Build it

PartHow
StandFoam-board box 30 cm tall, 20 × 15 cm, with one cross wall inside, all cut from one board. It carries the 2 kg jug about 30 times over. The electronics hide inside.
ColumnsThe two Libbey cylinders, either side of the stand. Mark the cap line 7.2 cm above the inside floor of each glass, which is 347 mL in this vase. Any marker or tape works.
SiphonsTwo tubes per column, about 1 m each, from the bottom of the jug, out through its neck, through the lever, down inside the column to its floor. Prime by filling the tubes, then pinch. One tube alone is too slow.
ValvesA craft-stick lever hinged at one end, both tubes under it 1 cm from the hinge, the servo pressing 3 cm out. That triples the servo's 12 to 18 N to about 35 to 50 N. Tune the closed angle until both tubes seal.
MetersUltrasonic sensor on a craft-stick bridge over each column, looking straight down at the water, which reflects sound well. The beam stays inside the glass at every level. If readings jump, float a 7 cm disc of foam-board scrap wrapped in tape.
GlowAn amber or yellow LED under each column lights the water while that valve is open.
WeatherHeat sensor on a short stalk between the stand and the Max Day column, clear of the water.
SpillsPaper towels or a lunch tray under the rig. Electronics stay inside the stand.
Arduino pinGoes to
D9Hybrid valve servo, signal
D10Max Day valve servo, signal
D2Heat sensor data (DHT11 or DHT22)
A0Or a thermistor, with a 10 kΩ divider
D4, D5Hybrid ultrasonic, trig and echo
D6, D7Max Day ultrasonic, trig and echo
D11, D12Column LEDs, each through 220 Ω
PowerSensors on the Arduino's 5 V. Servos on their own 5 V: a USB cable cut open to a phone charger or power bank, grounds tied together. A holding servo can pull about half an amp, enough to reset a board fed by USB.

What to expect. Each tube passes about 8 to 13 mL a second with 25 to 40 cm of head, so a column fills at 16 to 26 mL a second. The hybrid needs its valve open 22 to 34 seconds of the 60-second day and rises about 12 cm; Max Day needs 12 to 19 seconds and stops at about 7 cm. Calibrate once on site: time 100 mL through one valve and enter it in the app.

If something breaks

  1. Meter reads badly: run on valve time instead. The interlock counts milliseconds times measured flow.
  2. One valve won't seal: build one column and run the day twice, hybrid then Max Day.
  3. Heat sensor dies: the day still runs on the measured June 24 weather, without the live heat wave.
  4. The rig fails at judging: the app's story runs with no hardware. Nothing on screen depends on the USB cable.
